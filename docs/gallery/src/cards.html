Some people communicate with sounds, not words.

Many autistic people, and people with cerebral palsy or some genetic conditions, are minimally speaking. Their families learn what each sound means.

A new teacher, a substitute nurse, or a babysitter starts from nothing. Requests go unanswered. Distress gets read as misbehaviour.

Hum carries the family's knowledge to everyone else, on a phone, with no audio ever uploaded.

hum

Two layers: one measured, one honest.

What kind of sound

Objective. Same for everyone.
  • 11 types: laugh, cry, scream, cough, sneeze, sniff, sigh, throat clearing, yawn, breathing, other voice
  • 90.3% correct on 4,703 clips from people it never heard
  • Body sounds are named, never interpreted

What it means

Personal. Learned from the family.
  • Prototype per meaning, updated with one tap
  • Answers as a set sized by conformal prediction: one meaning when sure, two when torn
  • Upset alert from several sounds in a row

Raw audio, 16 kHz, into two small CNNs (5.7 MB each) running in the browser with ONNX. No server.
